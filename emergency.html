First Aid Protocols

What to Do During an Emergency

Essential steps to stabilize the patient while help is on the way.

Suspected Heart Attack

  • Have the patient sit down and rest quietly.
  • Loosen any tight clothing around the chest and neck.
  • If not allergic, chew an aspirin (300mg) after calling emergency.
  • Do not allow patient to drive or walk.

Stroke — Remember "FAST"

  • F (Face): Ask to smile; does one side droop?
  • A (Arms): Ask to raise both arms; does one drift downward?
  • S (Speech): Is speech slurred or strange?
  • T (Time): Call +91 98765 43210 immediately! Note the exact time.

Heavy Bleeding / Trauma

  • Apply direct, firm pressure over wound with a clean cloth.
  • Elevate the injured limb above heart level if no bone fracture.
  • Do not remove deeply embedded objects; pack around them.
  • Keep the patient warm to prevent shock.

Thermal / Chemical Burns

  • Cool the burn with cool running tap water for 15-20 minutes.
  • Never apply ice, butter, or home remedies.
  • Cover loosely with a sterile cloth or clean plastic film.
  • Rush to emergency for blistering or facial burns.

Seizures / Fits

  • Clear hard or sharp objects away from patient.
  • Turn patient gently onto one side (recovery position).
  • Never force anything into their mouth or hold them down.
  • Time the seizure and call ambulance.

Infant / Child Emergencies

  • High fever (>101°F) in an infant under 3 months is an emergency.
  • Chest retractions (breathing with ribs sinking) requires immediate ER.
  • Lethargy, refusal to feed, or continuous vomiting.
  • Bring child directly to our 24×7 pediatric emergency bay.
